> Don't read a paper — interrogate it. A paper is a claim; your job is to find out whether to believe it.

## Read in passes

1. **Skim (5 min):** title, abstract, figures, conclusion. What's the claim? Is it worth your time?
2. **Structure (20 min):** intro and method. What problem, what's new, what would you have to believe for this to work?
3. **Deep (as long as it takes):** derivations, experiments, and the fine print — for the few papers that matter to your project.

## Read actively

- Ask before each section: *what do I expect them to do next?* Being surprised is where you learn.
- Separate **claim**, **evidence**, and **your opinion** in your notes.
- Look for what's *not* shown — missing baselines, ablations, or failure cases.
- Try to state the core idea in one sentence a friend could understand. If you can't, keep reading.

## Keep notes you'll reuse

A short note per paper (idea, why it matters, what you'd steal, what you doubt) compounds into a personal knowledge base. See the site's [research notes](/personal_website/notes) for the style.

## In short

Read in passes, match depth to importance, and separate claim from evidence from opinion. Aim to explain, not just to finish.

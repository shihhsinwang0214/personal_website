> Your job on stage is to change what's in the audience's head — one clear idea they keep, not ten they forget.

## Design for the listener

- Decide the **one thing** you want everyone to remember, and build backward from it.
- Open with the problem and why anyone should care, before any method.
- One idea per slide; big visuals; few words. The slide supports you, not replaces you.
- Show intuition and a picture before equations.

## Deliver

Tell a story with a clear arc. Signpost ("three parts; here's the first"). Slow down at the key idea. It's fine to say "I don't know" in Q&A — then say how you'd find out. Practice out loud, at least once end-to-end, ideally in group meeting.

## Know your audience

A conference talk, a group-meeting WIP, and a job talk have different goals and depths. Calibrate.

## In short

Pick one idea, motivate before method, show intuition before math, and practice out loud. A talk is a gift, not a report.

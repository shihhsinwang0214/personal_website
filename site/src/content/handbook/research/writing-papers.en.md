> If you can't write it clearly, you don't understand it clearly yet. Writing is how we find the holes.

## Write early

Start the paper (or a one-page outline) long before the results are done. Writing exposes gaps in the story and often changes what experiments you run. A living draft is a research tool, not a final chore.

## Lead with the idea

A reader should get the core contribution from the title, abstract, intro, and figure 1 alone. State the problem, why it matters, what's new, and why it works — in that order, in plain language. Cut throat-clearing.

## Respect the reader

- One idea per paragraph; topic sentence first.
- Figures that carry the argument; captions that stand alone.
- Precise claims, honest limitations, and no overselling.
- Define notation on first use and reuse it consistently.

For the craft of the introduction specifically, see the note [How to Write a Compelling Introduction](/personal_website/notes/writing-compelling-introduction).

## Revise

The first draft is for you; later drafts are for the reader. Expect several passes. Read it aloud; cut 10%.

## In short

Write early to find the holes, lead with the idea, respect the reader, and revise ruthlessly. Clarity is a courtesy and a test.

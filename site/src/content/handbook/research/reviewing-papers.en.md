> A review is feedback to a fellow researcher, not a verdict on a person. Be tough on the work and generous to the author.

## How we review

- **Understand before judging.** State the paper's contribution in your own words first.
- **Be specific and constructive.** "Unclear" doesn't help; "Section 3.2's assumption seems to contradict Eq. 4 — could you clarify?" does.
- **Separate major from minor.** Distinguish "this changes the conclusion" from "typo on p.4."
- **Be fair about scope.** Review the paper that was written, not the one you'd have written.
- **Acknowledge strengths**, not only weaknesses.

## Ethics of reviewing

Reviewing is confidential: don't share, use, or discuss the ideas in a paper you're reviewing, and don't paste it into third-party AI tools ([AI Usage](/personal_website/handbook/ai-usage)). Declare conflicts of interest. If you're under-qualified for part of it, say so honestly.

## Why we do it

Reviewing sharpens your own judgment and your writing — you learn to see the moves that make a paper convincing. It's service to the community that reviewed your work too.

## In short

Understand first, be specific and fair, keep it confidential, and be kind. Review the way you'd want to be reviewed.

## Before you write

Three steps — skipping any of them weakens your email:

1. Browse the rest of the site — home, About, Publications, Notes — to see what we actually work on. A message that shows you know what you're applying to stands out immediately.
2. Read [Is This Lab Right for You?](/personal_website/handbook/is-this-lab-right-for-you) and [What I Look For](/personal_website/handbook/what-i-look-for).
3. Complete the [Research Reflection Questionnaire](/personal_website/handbook/research-reflection-questionnaire). **It is part of the application** — there are no correct answers and nothing is graded, but your responses will be the starting point of our first conversation.

## How to reach out

Email me at **{{ your-email }}** with:

- A short paragraph on what you're curious about and *why* — not a generic statement.
- One concrete thing you've done (a project, repo, note, or result) and what *you* contributed.
- Your answers to the [Research Reflection Questionnaire](/personal_website/handbook/research-reflection-questionnaire) (an attachment is fine; a few honest sentences per question is plenty).
- Your CV and, if relevant, transcript.
- For prospective PhD/master's students: the program and intended start term.

Please keep the email itself under a page (the questionnaire doesn't count). A focused note is more persuasive than a long one.

## What happens next

1. I read every message that is specific to the lab. If there's a fit, I'll reply to set up a chat.
2. A conversation (or two), starting from your questionnaire answers: your interests and a problem you've thought about. I'll usually also ask you to pick a mathematical tool you know well and explain it to me (see [Who Should Apply](/personal_website/handbook/who-should-apply)) — I care a lot about whether ideas can be made clear.
3. Sometimes a small, time-boxed exploration so we both learn how we work together.

Timelines depend on the season and openings — **{{ add current openings / deadlines }}**. If you don't hear back during a busy admissions window, a brief, polite follow-up is welcome.

## In short

Reflect first (the questionnaire), then be specific (one real thing), then tell me why this lab. That's the whole game.

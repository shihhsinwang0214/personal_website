> Potential is a trajectory, not a snapshot. I read for the slope, not just the intercept.

When I read an application I'm trying to answer one question: *will working together help you grow into an independent researcher?* A few things tell me more than grades.

## Signals that matter

- **Evidence of independent thinking.** A project you started, a question you chased on your own, a bug you refused to leave unsolved. Small is fine — I want to see you drive.
- **Depth over breadth.** One thing you understand deeply beats ten things you've touched. Tell me about the hard step, not the highlight reel.
- **How you handle not-knowing.** Can you say "I don't know" and then describe how you'd find out? That's more impressive than pretending.
- **Care and craft.** Clean code, a clear figure, a well-written paragraph — these show you take pride in the work.
- **Follow-through.** Did you finish? Did you iterate after the first version? Persistence is a research skill.

## What matters less than people think

Prestige of prior institution, a long list of skills, or having already "done ML." I'd rather see a physics student who reasons carefully than a checklist of frameworks.

## How to show these

Point me at something concrete — a repo, a note, a short write-up, a question you find interesting and *why*. A page of your real thinking beats a polished statement full of adjectives. See the [Application Process](/personal_website/handbook/application-process) for the specifics.

## In short

I look for curiosity, ownership, depth, and follow-through — the slope of your growth, not a snapshot of your résumé.

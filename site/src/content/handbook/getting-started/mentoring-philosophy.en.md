> My job is to make myself less necessary to you over time. Success is your independence.

I think of mentoring as raising your **ceiling**, not managing your **tasks**. That shapes how I show up.

## Guidance that fades on purpose

Early on I'll be hands-on: we'll pick problems together, meet often, and I'll model how I think. As you grow, I deliberately step back — fewer instructions, more questions — so that finding and framing problems becomes *your* skill, not mine. If it ever feels like I'm giving you less, that's usually a compliment.

## I optimize for your growth, not the paper

If a slower path teaches you more, we often take it. I'd rather you struggle productively for a week and truly own the idea than have me hand you the answer and own it for you. (When a deadline genuinely requires it, I'll say so and we'll switch modes.)

## What you can expect from me

- Honest, specific feedback — on the work, never on your worth.
- Time and attention, especially when you're stuck.
- Credit that reflects real contribution (see [Authorship Policy](/personal_website/handbook/authorship-policy)).
- Support for your goals, including ones that lead you out of academia.

## What I ask in return

Bring your thinking, not just your results. Tell me what you tried and why. Disagree with me when you have reasons — I learn from you too. And tell me early when something is wrong, technically or personally.

## In short

I mentor toward your independence: lots of scaffolding early, steadily removed, always in service of you becoming a researcher who no longer needs it.

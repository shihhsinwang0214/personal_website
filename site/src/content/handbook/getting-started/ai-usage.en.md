> Use AI to think faster, never to avoid thinking. You are always accountable for what carries your name.

AI tools (chat assistants, coding copilots, literature helpers) are genuinely useful and welcome here. The line that matters is simple: **they amplify your understanding; they don't substitute for it.**

## Good uses

- Explaining an unfamiliar concept or paper at your level, as a starting point.
- Boilerplate code, refactors, tests, and rubber-ducking a bug.
- Drafting, tightening, or translating prose you then own and check.
- Brainstorming directions you then evaluate critically.

## Use with care

- **Never trust facts, citations, math, or results without verifying them.** Models fabricate confidently. A citation you didn't read doesn't exist as far as we're concerned.
- **Don't paste unpublished ideas, private data, or confidential material** (e.g., papers you're reviewing) into third-party tools.
- **Don't let AI author claims you can't defend.** If you can't explain it without the tool, you don't understand it yet.

## Accountability and honesty

Anything with your name on it — code, a paragraph, a proof — is *your* responsibility, whether or not a tool helped write it. When AI use is material to a result or a paper, disclose it as venues require. Using AI to genuinely learn faster is great; using it to *look* competent while skipping understanding hurts only you.

## In short

AI is a power tool for thinking. Verify everything, protect what's confidential, and own every word that carries your name.

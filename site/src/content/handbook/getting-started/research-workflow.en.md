> Research is a loop, not a line: question → hypothesis → smallest experiment → evidence → update → repeat.

A rough rhythm that keeps projects healthy:

1. **Frame the question** so it's precise enough to be *wrong*. A vague question can't be answered.
2. **Predict** what you expect to see and why. Writing the prediction down first keeps you honest.
3. **Run the smallest experiment** that could change your mind. Prefer a one-hour probe over a one-week build.
4. **Look at the evidence** carefully — including the boring failure modes — and update your belief.
5. **Write as you go.** A running research log (dated notes, what you tried, what you saw) is the single highest-leverage habit here.
6. **Zoom out weekly.** What did I learn? What's the next most-informative thing to try?

Keep a lightweight log and a short "state of the project" you can share in one screen. See [Running Experiments](/personal_website/handbook/running-experiments) and [Reproducibility](/personal_website/handbook/reproducibility) for the how.

## In short

Small, fast, honest loops beat big, slow, hopeful ones. Write things down; update on evidence.

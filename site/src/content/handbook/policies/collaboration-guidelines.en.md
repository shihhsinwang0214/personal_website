> Good collaboration is generous by default and clear about ownership. Both at once.

## Inside the lab

- **Help freely; ask freely.** A lab is a compounding asset — a 15-minute unblock for a labmate is worth more than the same 15 minutes on your own task.
- **Be clear about ownership.** Each project has a driver who owns direction and decisions; collaborators support without taking over.
- **Share what you learn.** A trick, a bug, a good paper — post it. The lab gets smarter together.
- **Leave shared things better** — code, docs, datasets, the whiteboard.

## Collaborating across groups

External collaborations can be excellent — and they add coordination cost. Before starting one, get rough agreement on **goals, who does what, authorship expectations, and data/code sharing.** Writing this down early prevents almost every later dispute.

## Set expectations early

Most collaboration friction is really an unspoken-expectations problem: about credit, timelines, or scope. Name these at the start and revisit them; see [Authorship Policy](/personal_website/handbook/authorship-policy) and [Communication](/personal_website/handbook/communication).

Be generous, be clear about who drives, and agree on goals and credit up front. Most disputes are just expectations left unsaid.

> Data you can't find, trust, or trace is data you don't really have. Organize as if someone else will need it — because they will.

## Principles

- **Organized.** A predictable folder/naming scheme so anyone can find the right dataset and version.
- **Traceable.** Record where data came from and how it was processed. Provenance turns a number into evidence.
- **Backed up.** Important data and results live in more than one place. Assume any single disk will fail.
- **Access-controlled.** Sensitive or private data is stored and shared only as its agreements allow.
- **Reproducible pipelines.** Prefer scripts over manual steps so processing can be re-run and audited ([Reproducibility](/personal_website/handbook/reproducibility)).

## Handling sensitive data

Respect the terms of any dataset (licenses, IRB/data-use agreements). Don't move restricted data into personal accounts or third-party tools, including AI assistants ([AI Usage](/personal_website/handbook/ai-usage)). When unsure whether something is allowed, ask before you copy.

## When people leave

Projects outlive people. Before you move on, leave data, code, and a short README in a state a labmate can pick up. Continuity is a courtesy to the lab and to your own past work.

## In short

Organize, trace, back up, and control access; automate pipelines; hand off cleanly. Treat data as the shared, long-lived asset it is.

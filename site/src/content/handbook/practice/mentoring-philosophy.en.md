> My job is to make myself less necessary to you over time. The success I aim for is your independence. What I promise you and what I expect are laid out in full in [Promises and Expectations](/personal_website/handbook/promises-and-expectations).

I think of mentoring as raising your **ceiling**, not managing your **tasks**. That shapes how I show up in your research.

## Guidance that fades on purpose

Early on I'll be hands-on: we'll pick problems together, talk often, and I'll model how I think — how I use intuition to take a problem apart. As you grow, I deliberately step back — fewer instructions, more questions — so that finding and framing problems becomes *your* skill, not mine. If it ever feels like I'm giving you less, that's usually a compliment.

In one-on-ones, my role is to listen to your understanding and how you express it, give you direction and context, and clear obstacles early; the work stays mainly in your hands. If a discussion turns into me just issuing instructions one-way, that isn't a good sign to me (see [How the Day-to-Day Works](/personal_website/handbook/daily-practice)).

## I optimize for your growth, not the paper

If a slower path teaches you more, we often take it. I'd rather you struggle productively for a week and truly make an idea yours than have me hand you the answer and own it for you. But I'll also be strict about quality and assess a topic's potential early, so you don't waste time on something with no value. (When a deadline genuinely requires it, I'll say so and we'll switch pace.)

## What you can expect from me

- Honest, specific feedback — on the work, never on your worth.
- Time and attention, especially when you're stuck.
- Credit that reflects real contribution (see [Authorship and Research Contribution](/personal_website/handbook/authorship)).
- Support for your goals, including ones that lead you out of academia.

## What I ask in return

Bring your thinking, not just your results. Tell me what you tried and why you thought of it that way. Disagree with me when you have reasons — I learn from you too. And tell me early when something is wrong, technically or personally. Problems caught early are usually still small.

> Integrity is the one thing we never trade for a result. A career is long; trust, once lost, doesn't come back.

## The non-negotiables

- **Never fabricate or falsify.** Not data, not results, not citations. Report what happened, including the inconvenient parts.
- **No plagiarism.** Words, code, figures, and ideas get credited to their source. When in doubt, cite.
- **Report honestly.** Include failures, ablations that didn't help, and limitations. Cherry-picking is a form of lying — a failed result is data, it belongs in the failure log, not hidden.
- **Give fair credit.** Actual research contribution is the sole principle; see [Authorship and Research Contribution](/personal_website/handbook/authorship), and acknowledge help and prior work generously.
- **Respect confidentiality.** Papers under review, private data, and others' unpublished ideas stay private — and don't paste them into third-party AI tools (see [Using AI Tools](/personal_website/handbook/ai-usage)).

## Everyday integrity

Most ethics is not dramatic — it's the small choice to double-check a suspicious result, to say "I'm not sure," to redo the run you know was sloppy. Honesty with *yourself* about what you actually know is where it starts.

## If something feels wrong

Raise it — with me, or with the appropriate university channel if needed. Mistakes made honestly are fine and expected; hiding them is not. You will never be penalized for reporting a genuine error or concern.

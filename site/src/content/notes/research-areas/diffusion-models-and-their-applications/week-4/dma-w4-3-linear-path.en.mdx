# Gaussian Sources: Where DDIM and Flow Matching Meet

<Objectives title="After reading, you can">
- **Derive the VP interpolation velocity** as a combination of conditional data and noise means.
- **Convert noise, score, and velocity predictions** on the same probability path.
- **Relate DDIM to Flow Matching.** Their continuous dynamics agree on the corresponding VP path.
- **Identify additional design freedom:** the source distribution, reference path, and endpoint pairing.
</Objectives>

We asked whether a Gaussian-source Flow Matching field relates to DDIM's deterministic dynamics.

A denoiser predicts data or noise; a Flow Matching network predicts velocity. On the same noisy distribution, can these quantities be converted?

## Rewrite the diffusion path in Flow Matching notation

Here $t=0$ is noise and $t=1$ is data, opposite to the forward-corruption direction of Unit 1.

Use $\tau$ for data-to-noise time and $t$ for noise-to-data time. The earlier state was

$$
x_\tau=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_\tau}\,x_0+\sigma_\tau\epsilon.
$$

Reverse its interpretation and choose endpoints

$$
X_0=\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I),\qquad X_1\sim p_{\mathrm{data}},
$$

to write the interpolation as

$$
I_t=\alpha_tX_1+\sigma_tX_0
=\alpha_tX_1+\sigma_t\epsilon,\qquad
\alpha_0=0, \sigma_0=1, \alpha_1=1, \sigma_1=0.
$$

The coefficients have these meanings:

- $\alpha_t$ is the **signal amplitude**, corresponding to $\sqrt{\bar\alpha}$ in Unit 1. It is not the earlier one-step retention factor $\alpha_s=1-\beta_s$.
- $\sigma_t$ is the noise amplitude.
- A VP path satisfies $\alpha_t^2+\sigma_t^2=1$.

In this reversed direction, signal increases while noise decreases. Exact zero/one endpoints describe an ideal limiting path; finite diffusion schedules can retain a small prior mismatch.

Let us derive the velocity corresponding to this interpolation.

For fixed endpoint data and noise, differentiate the reference position:

$$
\dot I_t=\dot\alpha_tX_1+\dot\sigma_t\epsilon.
$$

The optimal marginal field is its conditional average:

$$
v_t^\star(x)
=\mathbb E[\dot I_t\mid I_t=x]
=\mathbb E[\dot\alpha_tX_1+\dot\sigma_t\epsilon\mid I_t=x].
$$

## Connect this field to the denoiser

Condition on the current position:

$$
x=\alpha_tX_1+\sigma_t\epsilon.
$$

For $\alpha_t>0$, solve for clean data:

$$
X_1=\frac{x-\sigma_t\epsilon}{\alpha_t}.
$$

Substitute into the reference velocity:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot I_t
&=\dot\alpha_tX_1+\dot\sigma_t\epsilon\\
&=\frac{\dot\alpha_t}{\alpha_t}x
+\left(
\dot\sigma_t-
\frac{\dot\alpha_t}{\alpha_t}\sigma_t
\right)\epsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$

Then average over compatible origins:

$$
v_t^\star(x)
=\frac{\dot\alpha_t}{\alpha_t}x
+\left(
\dot\sigma_t-
\frac{\dot\alpha_t}{\alpha_t}\sigma_t
\right)
\mathbb E[\epsilon\mid I_t=x].
$$

The conditional noise mean therefore determines velocity. At the matching noise level, the denoiser estimates

$$
\epsilon_\theta(x,t)
\approx\mathbb E[\epsilon\mid I_t=x]
=-\sigma_t\nabla_x\log p_t(x),
$$

Tweedie's identity gives the last equality. Substitution produces the conversion

$$
\boxed{
v_\theta(x,t)
=\frac{\dot\alpha_t}{\alpha_t}x
+\left(
\dot\sigma_t-
\frac{\dot\alpha_t}{\alpha_t}\sigma_t
\right)\epsilon_\theta(x,t).
}
$$

or, in score coordinates,

$$
v_t^\star(x)
=\frac{\dot\alpha_t}{\alpha_t}x
-\sigma_t\left(
\dot\sigma_t-
\frac{\dot\alpha_t}{\alpha_t}\sigma_t
\right)\nabla_x\log p_t(x).
$$

This is an algebraic conversion of an existing predictor, rather than training another network.

> **On the same VP interpolation, noise, score, and velocity encode convertible conditional-mean information.**

## What happens when a DDIM step becomes infinitesimal?

We have converted outputs. We still need to check whether this field is DDIM's underlying deterministic dynamics.

Return to data-to-noise time $\tau$. Deterministic DDIM [1] reconstructs a new level using the current clean and noise predictions:

$$
x_s
=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_s}\,\widehat x_0(x_\tau,\tau)
+\sigma_s\epsilon_\theta(x_\tau,\tau),
\qquad s<\tau.
$$

where

$$
\widehat x_0(x_\tau,\tau)
=\frac{x_\tau-\sigma_\tau\epsilon_\theta(x_\tau,\tau)}
{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_\tau}}.
$$

The current state also satisfies

$$
x_\tau
=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_\tau}\,\widehat x_0(x_\tau,\tau)
+\sigma_\tau\epsilon_\theta(x_\tau,\tau).
$$

Subtract the two times:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_s-x_\tau
&=\left(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_s}-\sqrt{\bar\alpha_\tau}\right)
\widehat x_0(x_\tau,\tau)\\
&\quad+(\sigma_s-\sigma_\tau)\epsilon_\theta(x_\tau,\tau).
\end{aligned}
$$

Divide by $s-\tau$ and take $s\to\tau$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm dx_\tau}{\mathrm d\tau}
&=\frac{\mathrm d\sqrt{\bar\alpha_\tau}}{\mathrm d\tau}
\widehat x_0(x_\tau,\tau)
+\dot\sigma_\tau\epsilon_\theta(x_\tau,\tau)\\
&=\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\tau}
\log\sqrt{\bar\alpha_\tau}\,x_\tau
+\left(
\dot\sigma_\tau-
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\tau}
\log\sqrt{\bar\alpha_\tau}\,\sigma_\tau
\right)
\epsilon_\theta(x_\tau,\tau).
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the same velocity conversion, expressed in the opposite time direction. Infinitesimal deterministic DDIM follows the corresponding Flow ODE.

## Which ODE does the VP schedule produce?

Use $\dot{\bar\alpha}_\tau=-\beta(\tau)\bar\alpha_\tau$ to differentiate the signal amplitude:

$$
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\tau}\log\sqrt{\bar\alpha_\tau}
=-\frac12\beta(\tau).
$$

Differentiating the noise variance gives

$$
2\sigma_\tau\dot\sigma_\tau
=\beta(\tau)\bar\alpha_\tau.
$$

Substitute the noise-score relation. The score coefficient becomes

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\sigma_\tau\left(
\dot\sigma_\tau+\frac12\beta(\tau)\sigma_\tau
\right)
&=-\frac12\beta(\tau)
\left(\bar\alpha_\tau+\sigma_\tau^2\right)\\
&=-\frac12\beta(\tau).
\end{aligned}
$$

and the continuous equation is

$$
\boxed{
\frac{\mathrm dx_\tau}{\mathrm d\tau}
=-\frac12\beta(\tau)x_\tau
-\frac12\beta(\tau)\nabla\log p_\tau(x_\tau).
}
$$

This is the **probability-flow ODE** [2]. Generation integrates $\tau$ from the noisy end toward the data end.

DDPM and DDIM share denoising supervision. Its conditional mean converts into the VP field; deterministic DDIM supplies a finite-step approximation to that continuous rule.

<Remark label="Caution" summary="Changing coordinates is not changing the path">
The conversion coefficients must match the interpolation used in training. Combining a VP-trained denoiser with arbitrary linear-path coefficients generally evaluates it on different intermediate distributions.

**Output conversion can avoid retraining; an arbitrary change of probability path cannot.**
</Remark>

## What additional freedom does Flow Matching provide?

DDIM already defines a deterministic flow. On the corresponding path, both constructions have the form

$$
\frac{\mathrm dX_t}{\mathrm dt}=v_t(X_t),
$$

Flow Matching additionally lets us choose

- The source distribution $p_0$.
- The reference path $I_t$.
- The joint pairing of source and target endpoints.

The simplest first choice is straight interpolation:

$$
I_t=(1-t)X_0+tX_1,\qquad
\dot I_t=X_1-X_0.
$$

Its reference velocity is constant. If generation followed a known reference line, one Euler step would suffice. Whether the averaged field has this property is a separate question.

| Choice | DDPM/DDIM setup | Flow Matching |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Gaussian prior | A chosen $p_0$ |
| Reference path | Noise schedule | Chosen interpolation |
| Pairing | Independent noise and data | Chosen endpoint joint |
| Output | Commonly noise or score | Commonly velocity, with other parametrizations possible |

Changing the path or endpoint coupling generally changes both intermediate distributions and the optimal field. Output-coordinate equivalence alone does not preserve the trained model.

<Remark label="Supplement" summary="Velocity MSE changes weights in noise coordinates">
For the linear path,

$$
\epsilon=I_t-t\dot I_t,\qquad
v_\theta(x,t)=\frac{x-\epsilon_\theta(x,t)}{t}.
$$

we obtain

$$
\|v_\theta-\dot I_t\|^2
=\frac1{t^2}\|\epsilon_\theta-\epsilon\|^2.
$$

Uniform-time velocity MSE therefore carries a $1/t^2$ weight when expressed as noise error. It is not identical to DDPM's uniform noise MSE.
</Remark>

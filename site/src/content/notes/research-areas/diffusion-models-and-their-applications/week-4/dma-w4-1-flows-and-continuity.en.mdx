## Suppose we know both endpoints

Replace sand with people leaving the New Year's fireworks near Taipei 101. Some head toward City Hall Station, others toward Taipei 101/World Trade Center or Xiangshan Station.

First suppose each person knows a starting point $x_0$ and destination $x_1$. Choose a curve with those endpoints:

$$
I_t(x_0,x_1).
$$

The simplest idealized route is a straight line:

$$
I_t(x_0,x_1)=(1-t)x_0+tx_1,\qquad
\dot I_t(x_0,x_1)=x_1-x_0.
$$

Given a pair of endpoints, both position and velocity are directly calculable. No network is needed.

<Remark label="Terminology" summary="Random endpoints turn reference paths into a stochastic interpolant">
For fixed endpoints, $I_t(x_0,x_1)$ is a reference path. Sampling random endpoints makes $I_t(X_0,X_1)$ a random process connecting their distributions, a simple **stochastic interpolant**.

We focus here on the reference-path interpretation. <Ref to="dma-unified-equation"/> develops the general construction.
</Remark>

## A natural attempt: follow the crowd

If we do not know the route, we might follow nearby people who do.

At position $x$ and time $t$, consider reference paths passing through that location then, and observe their velocities.

Each reference path supplies an input $(I_t,t)$ and label $\dot I_t$. Train a network by regression:

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CFM}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb E_{t,(X_0,X_1)}
\left[
\left\|v_\theta(I_t,t)-\dot I_t\right\|^2
\right].
}
$$

At the reference position, ask the model to match that person's velocity.

An endpoint pair and a time directly yield this training pair. We do not solve an ODE to produce it. Lipman and colleagues [1] call this **simulation-free training**.

Simulation-free means no ODE solve is needed to construct these training targets. Generation along the trained field still needs numerical integration.

Several reference paths can share the same input while supplying different velocities. A deterministic network must return one answer.

### Squared error performs the averaging

We have seen this conflict before: when squared error fits multiple labels at the same input, it selects their mean.

Apply <Ref to="math-mse-conditional-expectation"/> with input $(I_t,t)$ and label $\dot I_t$:

$$
\boxed{
v_t^*(x)
=
\mathbb E\!\left[
\dot I_t(X_0,X_1)
\mid I_t(X_0,X_1)=x
\right].
}
$$

In the crowd example, this means:

> **At $x$, average the directions and speeds of people who could pass here at time $t$, using their conditional probabilities.**

Squared-error training and conditional averaging are two views of the same construction: one is the practical objective, the other its ideal field.

## How do we follow the trained field?

Draw $x^{(0)}\sim p_0$ and solve

$$
\frac{\mathrm dx_t}{\mathrm dt}=v_\theta(x_t,t).
$$

There is generally no closed-form solution. Divide time into intervals and approximate with a numerical solver. Euler uses

$$
x^{(k+1)}
=
x^{(k)}+\Delta t\,
v_\theta\!\left(x^{(k)},t_k\right).
$$

Ask for the current direction, walk a short distance, then ask again at the new location. Euler needs one network evaluation per step. Finer grids usually improve accuracy under the usual regularity conditions, at greater cost.

If velocity barely changes, a large step may work. If its direction or magnitude changes, frequent updates become necessary. Unit 3 asks whether we can reduce those changes as well as improve the solver.
